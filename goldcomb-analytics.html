Goldcomb Analytics

Customer behaviour, data quality & revenue reconciliation

A SQL-based investigation into customer activity, data inconsistencies, refund anomalies and discrepancies between Sales and Finance revenue reporting.

Key findings

Insight 01 · Data quality

Duplicate customer accounts

A case-insensitive email comparison revealed two customer accounts sharing the same email address. One account had six completed orders, while the other had no purchase history.

This suggests a potential duplicate registration that could distort customer counts and customer-level performance metrics.

Insight 02 · Data completeness

Missing customer regions

Data quality checks identified 37 customer records with missing region information.

Incomplete geographical data may affect regional segmentation, reporting accuracy and targeted marketing decisions.

Insight 03 · Refund anomalies

Potential duplicate refunds

55 suspected duplicate refund pairs were identified across the legacy and new systems, sharing the same order IDs and refund amounts.

The pattern suggests a potential duplication issue associated with system migration, which could overstate reported refunds and understate net revenue.

Transaction-level validation is required before confirming duplicate payments.

Insight 04 · Customer behaviour

Customer activity and inactivity

Using a 90-day activity window and completed orders, 8,362 of 49,181 non-test customers were classified as active, representing approximately 17% of the customer base.

The remaining 40,819 customers were classified as inactive, highlighting an opportunity for targeted customer reactivation campaigns.

Insight 05 · September 2026

Finding the culprit behind the refund spike

The Drip-Free Glass Honey Dispenser was associated with 77 refunded orders, representing $3,196 in refund amounts.

Of these, 59 were associated with a leaking lid, accounting for $2,448 — approximately 77% of the associated refund value.

This recurring complaint suggests a potential product quality issue that warrants investigation with the supplier.

Insight 06 · Financial reporting

Finance vs. Sales revenue reconciliation

September 2026 revenue reporting revealed a discrepancy between Sales revenue of $299,482 and Finance net revenue of $258,721.

The investigation examined reporting boundaries, order statuses and refund adjustments to understand the $40,761 difference.

The findings highlight the importance of consistent revenue definitions, reporting cutoffs and refund validation across business departments.

From analysis to action

Business recommendations

  • Implement customer deduplication and email validation.
  • Improve customer data completeness.
  • Validate refund transactions across source systems.
  • Develop targeted customer reactivation campaigns.
  • Investigate recurring product quality complaints.
  • Standardise revenue definitions across departments.

Analytical limitations

Potential duplicate refunds require transaction-level confirmation. Product-associated refunds may not represent refunds exclusively attributable to that product in multi-item orders. The detailed revenue reconciliation requires further validation.
